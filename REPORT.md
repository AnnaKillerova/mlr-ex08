Adding transforms.ColorJitter alongside geometric augmentation produced the largest single improvement in camera robustness on the custom LeafNetBN (B1), camera-like validation accuracy jumped from 55,9% to 73,1% (+17,2 percentage points) while clean validation accuracy remained stable at 73,1%. Combining ColorJitter with a CosineAnnealingLR scheduler over 30 epochs further raised B1's clean validation accuracy to 79,0% (+5,4 percentage points) and camera-like accuracy to 72,0% (+16,1 percentage points), while keeping the best epoch weights with ColorJitter reached 79,6% on clean validation. For the pretrained MobileNetV3-Small (B2), training for 30 epochs with CosineAnnealingLR improved clean accuracy from 77,4% to 83,9% (+6,5 percentage points) and camera-like accuracy from 70,4% to 76,3% (+5,9 percentage points). When ColorJitter was combined with CosineAnnealingLR and 30 epochs on MobileNetV3-Small, camera-like validation accuracy reached an overall best of 81,7% (+11,3 percentage points over B2 and +25,8 percentage points over B1) with 84,4% on clean validation. Comparing transfer learning strategies, freezing the pretrained backbone and training only the final linear layer (B2 + frozen head only) achieved 78,5% on clean validation and 75,8% on camera-like validation, but dropped to 42,8% on the historical set compared to 55,6% for full fine-tuning, showing that adapting the convolutional features is necessary for cross-session generalization. Finally, adding the historical dataset to training dramatically improved accuracy on the historical validation set (from 46,7% to 80,8% for B1 and from 55,6% to 85,8% for B2 with augmentation), because the models learned the visual style of last year's photo sessions.

Because our validation sets contain 62 images each, a single image accounts for 1,61% of accuracy, and the standard deviation across the three seeds typically ranges between 0,9% and 6,5% (reaching up to 10,4% on B1 + historical train). Small changes within ±1,61% of the baseline—such as AdamW (+0,5% on camera validation), label smoothing (+0,5% on clean validation), keeping the best epoch alone (+0.5% on camera validation), or extending B1 to 30 cosine epochs without colour jitter (+1,1% on camera validation)—fall entirely inside single-image resolution and inter-seed noise. By contrast, the improvements from ColorJitter on B1 (+17,2% camera validation), B2 + ColorJitter + cosine + 30 ep (+11,3% camera validation and +7,0% clean validation), B2 + aug + cosine + 30 ep (+8,6% clean validation and +7,0% camera validation), and the historical dataset's boost on historical validation (+30,3% to +34,2%) exceed both the 1,61% step size and the seed-to-seed standard deviation, representing genuine, reproducible improvements.

Replacing flattening with global average pooling (B1 + global avg pooling) severely degraded performance on our small four-layer network, dropping clean validation accuracy by 11,3 percentage points (to 62,4%) and camera-like accuracy by 7,0 percentage points (to 48,9%), because collapsing the 6x6 feature maps discarded spatial structure that the shallow classifier relied on. Adding dropout (p=0,25) to B1 also hurt clean validation accuracy by 5,9 percentage points (down to 67,7%) with zero improvement on camera-like validation (55,9%), as the small model already had strong geometric augmentation and underfitted when regularized further. Using AdamW with weight decay (0,01), label smoothing (0,1), or CosineAnnealingLR for 30 epochs without ColorJitter yielded no meaningful gains over the B1 baseline. Additionally, training on the combined original and historical datasets slightly reduced performance on our group's own clean and camera-like validation sets (dropping B2+aug from 77,4% to 71,0% on clean validation and from 74,7% to 69,9% on camera validation), indicating a domain shift between last year's camera conditions and our current dataset.

I selected B2 + ColorJitter + cosine + 30 ep (MobileNetV3-Small fine-tuned for 30 epochs with geometric and colour-jitter augmentation and a cosine annealing learning-rate schedule) for deployment on the phone. This model achieved the highest accuracy on the camera-like validation set (81,7% ± 1,9%, reaching 83,9% on seed 2 and 80,6% on seed 0) with low variance across seeds, while maintaining 84,4% clean validation accuracy. Unlike the models trained with the historical archive, it did not suffer a domain-shift drop on our group's leaf photographs. I exported this winning model (FINAL B2 + ColorJitter + cosine + 30 ep, 83,87% clean / 80,65% camera-like accuracy) to leaf_model.onnx with embedded model_info metadata and verified it in the course camera web app on GitHub Pages, where it ran smoothly in the browser and classified live camera leaf images accurately.
